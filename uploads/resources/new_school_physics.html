Chapter 1: Measurement & Physical Quantities

Physics is the study of matter in relation to energy. In science, quantities that can be measured are called physical quantities. They are divided into two fundamental categories:

  • Fundamental Quantities: Basic physical quantities that do not depend on other quantities. Examples: Length (metre, m), Mass (kilogram, kg), Time (second, s), Electric Current (ampere, A), Temperature (kelvin, K).
  • Derived Quantities: Quantities derived from the fundamental quantities by combination. Examples: Velocity (\(\text{m/s}\)), Acceleration (\(\text{m/s}^2\)), Force (Newton, N), Work & Energy (Joule, J).
Key Principle: All valid physical equations must be dimensionally homogeneous. The dimensions of terms on both sides of an equality sign must be identical.

Chapter 2: Motion and Kinematics

Motion is change of position of a body with time relative to a fixed reference point. Linear motion under uniform acceleration is governed by the three classic equations of motion:

  1. \( v = u + at \)
  2. \( s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \)
  3. \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as \)

Where \( u \) = initial velocity, \( v \) = final velocity, \( a \) = acceleration, \( t \) = time, and \( s \) = displacement.
